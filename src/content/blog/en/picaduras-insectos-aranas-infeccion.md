# Bug and Spider Bites: When a Bite Gets Infected

You took an evening walk along Brays Bayou, watered the yard barefoot, or hauled old boxes out of the garage. A little later, a welt shows up. Most of the time that's the whole story: it itches, turns pink and slowly fades. Every so often, though, that small mark takes a different turn. It gets warm, hurts more each time you look at it and starts to leak yellowish fluid. That turn is what this article is about: how to tell whether your bite is still just a bite or has become a skin infection.

## It's rarely the bug itself

Most of the insects around Houston don't push dangerous bacteria into your skin. Trouble starts once the skin breaks open. You scratch in your sleep, pop a blister with a fingernail or rub it raw under your work clothes, and the germs that normally live on the surface of the skin find an open door.

That's why a mosquito bite, which should cause nothing more than an itch, can end up with honey-colored crusts or a lump full of pus. It's also why people living with diabetes, poor circulation or very dry skin should keep a closer eye on their bites: an infection can move along in them without much warning.

## What to expect, and what goes wrong, bug by bug

### Mosquitoes

Around Meyerland they hang out near the bayous and wherever water sits in flowerpots and old tires, even in the fall while the weather stays mild. They leave an itchy bump that flattens out on its own. The risk lies in scratching: lines of open scratch marks are where infection usually begins.

### Fire ants

They burn right away, and a short while later a small white blister forms at each spot. That little bump isn't infected pus: it's part of the reaction to the venom, and the inside is clean. Popping it is what opens the way for bacteria. Leave it alone and keep it covered.

### Bees and wasps

The pain is sharp at first and the area swells. If the stinger stayed in, scrape it out sideways with the edge of a card instead of pinching it with your fingers. A large swollen patch around the sting can be just a strong skin reaction; what matters more is what happens in the rest of your body, covered further down.

### Bed bugs

They tend to leave several bites grouped together or in a row on the arms, neck or back, and you often notice them when you wake up. They itch for days, and the repeated scratching is what gets infected most. Besides caring for your skin, you'll need to clean the mattress and bedding, or the bites keep coming back night after night.

### Spiders

The vast majority of house spiders cause nothing serious. Two found in Texas are worth knowing about, without panicking:

- **Black widow:** shiny black, with a red hourglass mark on the belly. The bite may feel like a faint pinprick, but strong cramps, muscle pain in the belly or back, sweating and feeling unwell can follow. That's a trip to the emergency room.
- **Brown recluse:** brown, with a violin-shaped mark on its back; it likes dark corners, closets and stored boxes. At first the bite may not even hurt. Over the following days a blister can form, with a center that darkens or opens into a sore. Any wound like that should be looked at soon.

Often nobody actually saw a spider, and a "spider bite" turns out to be something else, such as an abscess. So how the spot looks and how it changes matters more than guessing what caused it.

## Normal reaction, infection or emergency: telling them apart

| What you see | What it usually means | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Itchy welt, pink only around the spot, calming down | Normal reaction to the bite | Home care, and no scratching |
| Redness that grows from one day to the next, warm skin, worsening pain, pus or yellow crusts | Skin infection | Have the medical team check it |
| A hard, painful lump that softens and fills with pus | Possible abscess | Get it evaluated to see if it needs draining |
| Red streaks running up from the bite, or red skin spreading fast with a fever | Infection on the move | Emergency room or 911 |
| Swelling of the face, lips or tongue, trouble breathing, dizziness | Severe allergic reaction | Call 911 right away |

One simple trick: trace the edge of the red area with a pen and snap a photo with your phone. If by the next morning the redness has crossed the line, it's no longer just a welt.

## What actually helps at home early on

1. **Wash with soap and water** as soon as you can, without scrubbing hard.
2. **Apply something cold**, a damp towel or ice wrapped in a cloth, in short stretches, to ease swelling and itching.
3. **Trim your nails**, and if you scratch in your sleep, cover the bite with gauze at night.
4. **Don't pop blisters** or try to dig anything out with needles or blades.
5. **Skip harsh home remedies:** bleach, gasoline, garlic or homemade pastes on broken skin do more harm than good.

If the itch keeps you up at night or the bite doesn't look right, ask at the clinic what to use instead of trying things at random.

## When not to wait: call 911

- Swelling of the face, lips or tongue, a tight throat or trouble breathing after a sting or bite.
- Dizziness, fainting or a racing heart.
- Red skin spreading quickly along with fever or chills.
- Red streaks running up the arm or leg from the bite.
- Strong cramps, heavy sweating or confusion after a possible black widow bite.

If you've ever had a severe reaction to a bee or wasp, bring it up at your next visit so you have a plan in case it happens again.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the white bump from a fire ant an infection?** Not necessarily. It's part of the reaction and the inside is clean; it becomes a problem if you pop it and the area turns red, warm and full of pus.

**How do I know if it was a spider?** Often you can't. What helps is watching how the spot changes: if the center darkens, it opens up or it hurts more and more, get it checked.

**Can I squeeze the bite to get the pus out?** Better not. Squeezing pushes the infection deeper and tears the skin; if pus has collected, it gets drained cleanly at the clinic.
