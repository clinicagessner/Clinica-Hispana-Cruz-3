# Vaginal Itching and Discharge: Yeast and Other Causes

It usually starts as something easy to brush off: an itch that shows up at the end of the day, a mild sting when you pat yourself dry, a mark on your underwear that doesn't look like the usual one. Plenty of women put up with it for weeks, out of embarrassment or because they figure "it'll go away." Sometimes it does. Other times it keeps coming back, and every attempt to fix it at home makes things a little messier. This article walks through the most common causes of vaginal itching and discharge, how to tell them apart and when it makes sense to get checked.

## First things first: discharge isn't automatically a bad sign

The vagina produces discharge every day. That's how it cleans itself and stays moist. Healthy discharge is clear or whitish, without a strong smell, and it shifts over the month: stretchier and more abundant mid-cycle, thicker before your period. It also increases during pregnancy and can change when you use hormonal birth control.

What deserves attention isn't having discharge but a clear change from what's normal for you: a different color, a different texture, a new smell, or itching, burning or redness that comes along with it.

## The three causes we see most

### Yeast (candidiasis)

This is the classic cause of intense itching. A fungus that normally lives in the body overgrows when the balance down there shifts. It tends to cause:

- Strong itching of the vulva and vaginal opening, often worse at night.
- Thick white discharge that looks a bit like cottage cheese, usually without a bad smell.
- Red, swollen skin, sometimes with tiny cracks that sting when you pee.

### Bacterial vaginosis

Here the culprit isn't a fungus but an imbalance among the bacteria that live in the vagina: the protective ones drop and others take their place. The main clue is the smell:

- Thin, watery discharge that's grayish or white.
- A strong, fishy odor, more noticeable after your period or after a shower.
- Little or no itching.

### Irritation from products or clothing

Not everything is an infection. The skin of the vulva is delicate and reacts to scented soaps, wet wipes, feminine sprays, fabric softeners, scented panty liners, spandex that doesn't breathe, or damp gym clothes left on for hours. The result is burning, itching and redness, sometimes with little change in discharge.

Douching deserves its own warning: it washes away the protective bacteria inside and clears the way for yeast or vaginosis. The inside of the vagina doesn't need washing.

## Telling them apart at a glance

| What you notice | Yeast | Bacterial vaginosis | Irritation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Itching | Intense | Mild or none | Moderate, with burning |
| Discharge | White, thick, clumpy | Gray or white, watery | Barely changes |
| Odor | Little or none | Strong, fishy | None |
| Extra clue | After antibiotics or with high sugar | Worse after douching | New product or clothing |

This table points you in a direction, but it doesn't diagnose. Symptoms overlap, and one woman can have two causes at once. That's why guessing from your bathroom at home misses so often.

## Things that tip the balance

Some situations make it more likely that the vaginal flora gets out of balance:

- **Antibiotics.** While treating a throat, ear or urinary infection, they also cut back the protective bacteria in the vagina, and yeast moves into the gap. That's why itching often starts right as the course ends.
- **Diabetes or poorly controlled blood sugar.** Extra glucose feeds yeast growth. Infections that keep returning are sometimes the first hint that sugar is running high.
- **Hormonal changes.** Pregnancy, hormonal birth control and menopause change discharge and how sensitive the area feels; after menopause, dryness alone can cause burning.
- **Heat and humidity.** In Houston, tight clothes and all-day sweat create the damp setting yeast loves.
- **Lowered defenses,** from an illness or long stretches of stress.

## The trouble with treating yourself blind

Pharmacy shelves carry over-the-counter yeast treatments, and many women reach for one at the first itch. The risk is that if it wasn't yeast, the product fixes nothing, irritates the skin further and delays the right treatment. Bacterial vaginosis, for example, won't get better with a yeast product.

Get checked instead of repeating the same remedy if:

- It's the first time this has happened and you're not sure what it is.
- You already tried something from the pharmacy and didn't improve.
- Symptoms come back several times a year.
- There's a pregnancy, confirmed or suspected, in the picture.
- You have diabetes, or the discharge comes with a strong odor.

## Habits that help keep it from coming back

1. Wash the outside only with water or a mild, unscented soap, and dry well.
2. Skip douching, feminine sprays and scented wipes.
3. Choose cotton underwear and change out of wet or sweaty clothes soon.
4. Sleep without tight underwear if itching bothers you at night.
5. If you're prescribed antibiotics and tend to get yeast afterward, mention it at the visit.
6. If you have diabetes, keeping your sugar on target also protects your intimate health.

## When it's an emergency

Intimate itching rarely puts your life at risk; still, these signs call for 911 or the ER without waiting for a culture:

- High fever with strong pain low in the belly.
- Sudden, severe pain in the pelvis.
- Heavy bleeding with dizziness or feeling faint.
- Pregnancy, or a possible pregnancy, with bleeding or sharp pain on one side.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it normal to have discharge every day?** Yes. Clear or whitish discharge without a strong smell or itching is part of how a healthy vagina works.

**Why do I get a yeast infection every time I take antibiotics?** Because the antibiotic also cuts back the protective bacteria in the vagina. If it happens often, bring it up at the visit before your next course starts.

**Can I be examined while I'm on my period?** If the unusual discharge or discomfort worries you, don't wait for your period to end: come in and the medical team will examine you anyway.
