# Fatigue, Low Mood and Low Desire in Men: When to Check Your Hormones

There is a scene that plays out in a lot of homes on the southwest side of Houston. He gets home from work, sits down in front of the TV and stays there. He no longer feels like going out on Sundays, snaps over little things and, for a while now, intimacy with his partner has slipped down the list. He sums it up in three words: "it's my age." His partner suspects there is more to it.

Sometimes it really is just a rough stretch. But when tiredness, a short temper and low desire show up together and hang around for weeks, it is worth looking for the cause instead of toughing it out. In this article the medical team at Clínica Hispana Cruz #3 explains why these three symptoms tend to travel together, what most often sets them off, and when it makes sense to check your hormones.

## Why these three symptoms rarely come alone

Energy, mood and sex drive rely on the same systems in the body: rest, blood sugar, circulation, the thyroid, male hormones and emotional health. When one of those gears slips, the others feel it. A man who sleeps poorly gets irritable; a man who is irritable and drained loses interest; and low desire, in turn, pulls his mood down even further. It is a loop.

That is why guessing doesn't help. Putting a name to the cause is what lets you break the loop.

## What is most often behind it

| Possible cause | Clues that make it more likely | How it gets sorted out |
|---|---|---|
| Short or broken sleep | Loud snoring, breathing pauses your partner notices, waking up tired | Bring it up at the visit; sometimes a sleep study outside the clinic is needed |
| Ongoing stress | Double shifts, debt, worry about family far away | An honest conversation; it doesn't always need lab work |
| Depression | Sadness or emptiness most days, no longer enjoying things you used to | Questions during the visit and a support plan |
| Slow thyroid | Feeling cold, dry skin, weight gain without eating more, sluggishness | Thyroid blood test |
| Anemia or low B12 | Pale skin, getting winded on stairs, tingling | Complete blood count and B12 level |
| High blood sugar | Strong thirst, urinating often, blurry vision | Glucose and A1C |
| High blood pressure and circulation | Headaches, changes in erections | Blood pressure reading and a heart check |
| Low male hormones | Less strength, a bigger belly, fewer morning erections | The visit decides which lab test is needed |

Notice something: most of these causes are not hormonal. Plenty of men walk in convinced they are "low on hormones," and what turns up is uncontrolled blood sugar, a lazy thyroid or months of five-hour nights. That is why a good evaluation looks at the whole picture before zeroing in on a single test.

## When does it make sense to check your hormones?

There is no exact age, but some signs make the question more reasonable:

- Your desire has dropped clearly and steadily, not just during a stressful season.
- You have fewer spontaneous morning erections, or they are harder to keep.
- You are losing strength or muscle even though you are as active as before.
- Your belly has grown and the tiredness doesn't lift even when you sleep well on your days off.
- Other causes such as blood sugar, thyroid or anemia have been ruled out or are being treated, and the symptoms remain.

If two or more of these sound like you, say so at the visit in those same words. Based on what you share and what the exam shows, the medical team decides which lab tests are needed and explains what each one is for. Checking hormones blindly, without looking at everything else, can lead to the wrong conclusions.

## Two weeks of notes before you come in

Arriving with details helps far more than arriving with "I just feel off." For about 14 days, jot down on your phone:

1. What time you go to bed, what time you get up and how many times you wake up.
2. Whether anyone at home has told you that you snore or stop breathing in your sleep.
3. Your mood for the day in one word: good, so-so, irritable, sad.
4. How many alcoholic drinks and how many sodas or energy drinks you have.
5. Whether your interest in intimacy faded little by little or all at once, and since when.
6. Any medications, vitamins or "for men" products you are using, even natural ones.

That last point matters: some treatments for other conditions can affect desire or energy, and nobody knows until someone reviews the full list. Don't stop anything on your own; bring it written down.

## If your mood is very low

There is a point where tiredness stops being just tiredness. If you feel nothing is worth it, that you would be a burden to your family, or thoughts of hurting yourself cross your mind, don't wait for an appointment: call or text 988, the suicide and crisis line, which also answers in Spanish around the clock. If you are in immediate danger, call 911. Reaching out at that moment takes courage, not weakness.

## When it can't wait

Get emergency care or call 911 if, along with the fatigue, any of these happen:

- Pressure or tightness in the chest that spreads to the arm, jaw or back.
- Shortness of breath that doesn't settle when you sit still.
- Weakness on one side of the body, a drooping face or trouble speaking.
- A painful erection that won't go down after several hours.
- Fainting or sudden confusion.
